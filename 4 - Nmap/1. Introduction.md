# 1. Introduction

## What is Nmap ?

- Nmap is an **open-source network scanning and discovery tool** released under **GNU GPL license**.
- Used by administrators and security professionals to:
    - Discover hosts
    - Monitor network exposure
    - Troubleshoot TCP/IP systems
- Created by **Gordon “Fyodor” Lyon** and maintained by a global community.
- Cross-platform: works on Linux, Windows, macOS.
- Continuously evolving since 1997.

---

## What Nmap Really Is !

- Nmap ≠ only port scanner.
- It interrogates networks to understand:
    - Open services
    - System responses
    - Exposure surface
- Helps read **how a network behaves**, not just what is open.
- Nmap observes and analyzes; it does not exploit systems.

**Mindset:**

Nmap = reading network body language.

---

## Why Nmap Still Dominates in 2026+

Despite new technologies:

- TCP/IP networking fundamentals remain unchanged.
- Firewalls, ports, routing, and services still work the same.
- Cloud and containers still rely on network protocols underneath.
- Used by attackers and defenders alike.
- Mature detection and fingerprint databases improve accuracy.

**Reality:**

Technology evolves, but network fundamentals don’t.

---

## Evolution of Nmap Capabilities

Key advancements over time include:

- Improved service & OS detection
- Cross-platform support improvements
- Nmap Scripting Engine (NSE) automation
- Ndiff for scan comparison
- Zenmap graphical interface
- Multi-language localization
- Continuous performance improvements

Nmap evolved from a simple scanner into a **complete network analysis suite**.

## Legal & Ethical Boundaries (Critical Section)

- Nmap itself is legal.
- Unauthorized scanning may violate laws or policies.
- Always scan with permission or defined scope.

Allowed examples:

- Personal labs
- Bug bounty authorized targets
- Company-approved testing

Risky examples:

- Random internet scanning
- Government or enterprise networks without authorization
- Curiosity-based scans

**Important Reality:**

Many attackers get detected during scanning, not exploitation.

**Golden Rule:**

Authorization decides legality.
