# 1.1 What is Cyber Security?

### Definition

Cyber Security is the practice of protecting computers, mobile devices, networks, applications, and data from cyber attacks, hackers, and unauthorized access.

### Simple Explanation

Today, almost everything is connected to the internet, such as banking, shopping, social media, and online learning. Cyber Security helps protect this digital information from being stolen, damaged, or misused.

In simple words, Cyber Security keeps our digital world safe.

### Example

Just like we lock our house to stop thieves, we use passwords, firewalls, and antivirus software to protect our computers and online accounts.

---

# 1.2 What Does Cyber Security Protect?

Cyber Security protects different parts of the digital world.

## 1. Data Protection

### Definition

Data Protection means keeping important information safe from unauthorized access.

### Explanation

Personal and business information should only be accessed by authorized users.

### Examples

- Passwords
- Bank details
- Personal photos
- Company documents

---

## 2. Systems Protection

### Definition

Systems Protection means securing computers, servers, and networks from attacks.

### Explanation

If a system is hacked, important services may stop working or data may be lost.

### Example

Protecting a company server from hackers.

---

## 3. Applications Protection

### Definition

Application Security protects software and mobile applications from cyber attacks.

### Explanation

Hackers often target applications to steal user information or money.

### Examples

- Banking Apps
- WhatsApp
- Instagram
- Amazon

---

## 4. Financial Assets Protection

### Definition

Financial Protection secures online money and digital transactions.

### Explanation

Cyber Security helps prevent online fraud and unauthorized payments.

### Examples

- UPI
- Credit Cards
- Net Banking

---

## 5. People Protection

### Definition

People Protection keeps users safe from online scams and identity theft.

### Explanation

Cyber criminals often trick people instead of attacking computers directly.

### Examples

- Fake OTP calls
- Phishing emails
- Social media scams

---

# 1.3 Why is Cyber Security Important?

### Definition

Cyber Security is important because most personal and business activities are now performed online.

### Simple Explanation

Without proper security, hackers can steal money, leak personal information, damage systems, or stop business operations.

Cyber Security protects users, organizations, and governments from these risks.

### Example

A bank uses Cyber Security to protect customer accounts and online transactions.

---

# 1.4 Common Cyber Attacks

Cyber attacks are methods used by attackers to steal information, damage systems, or gain unauthorized access.

---

## 1. Phishing

### Definition

Phishing is a social engineering attack where attackers trick users into sharing sensitive information.

### Simple Explanation

Attackers send fake emails, SMS, or websites that look real. Users unknowingly enter their passwords or bank details.

### Example

"You must verify your bank account immediately. Click here."

---

## 2. Malware

### Definition

Malware is harmful software designed to damage or control a computer system.

### Simple Explanation

Malware enters a system through infected files, websites, or email attachments.

### Example

Installing software from an unknown website that contains malicious code.

### Types of Malware

### Virus

Attaches itself to files and spreads when the infected file is opened.

**Example:** An infected USB drive spreads the virus to another computer.

---

### Worm

Spreads automatically through a network without user action.

**Example:** One infected computer infects all computers on the same network.

---

### Trojan

Looks like a genuine application but secretly performs harmful activities.

**Example:** A fake game that installs spyware in the background.

---

### Spyware

Secretly collects user information without permission.

**Example:** Recording passwords or browsing history.

---

## 3. Ransomware

### Definition

Ransomware is malware that encrypts files and demands money to unlock them.

### Simple Explanation

The attacker locks all important files and asks the victim to pay a ransom.

### Example

A company's files become inaccessible until payment is made.

---

## 4. Data Breach

### Definition

A Data Breach occurs when unauthorized users gain access to confidential information.

### Simple Explanation

Sensitive information is leaked, stolen, or exposed without permission.

### Example

A website accidentally leaks thousands of user passwords.

---

# 1.5 CIA Triad

The CIA Triad is the foundation of Cyber Security.

It consists of three important security principles.

---

## 1. Confidentiality

### Definition

Only authorized users should be able to access information.

### Example

Only you should be able to see your online banking account.

---

## 2. Integrity

### Definition

Data should remain accurate and should not be changed without permission.

### Example

Your bank balance should never change without a valid transaction.

---

## 3. Availability

### Definition

Systems and data should always be available when needed.

### Example

Hospital servers should remain online 24×7 for emergency services.

---

# 1.6 Types of Cyber Security

Cyber Security is a broad field that includes different areas of protection. Each type focuses on securing a specific part of the digital world.

---

## 1. Network Security

### Definition

Network Security protects computer networks from unauthorized access, attacks, and data theft.

### Simple Explanation

It monitors and controls network traffic to prevent hackers from entering the network. Firewalls, VPNs, and IDS/IPS are commonly used to secure networks.

### Example

A company uses a firewall to block hackers from accessing its internal network.

---

## 2. Application Security

### Definition

Application Security protects software and web applications from security vulnerabilities and cyber attacks.

### Simple Explanation

Developers use secure coding practices and security testing to remove bugs and vulnerabilities before hackers can exploit them.

### Example

Testing an online banking application to find SQL Injection or Cross-Site Scripting (XSS) vulnerabilities.

---

## 3. Information Security (InfoSec)

### Definition

Information Security protects sensitive information from unauthorized access, modification, or loss.

### Simple Explanation

Its main goal is to maintain the **Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability (CIA Triad)** of information.

### Example

Encrypting customer records so only authorized employees can access them.

---

## 4. Cloud Security

### Definition

Cloud Security protects data, applications, and services stored on cloud platforms.

### Simple Explanation

Many companies store their data on cloud services like AWS, Azure, or Google Cloud. Cloud Security helps protect this data from cyber threats.

### Example

Securing files stored on Google Drive or Amazon Web Services (AWS).

---

## 5. Endpoint Security

### Definition

Endpoint Security protects devices connected to a network.

### Simple Explanation

Every laptop, desktop, mobile phone, or tablet connected to a network is called an endpoint. Antivirus and Endpoint Detection & Response (EDR) tools help keep these devices secure.

### Example

Installing antivirus software on a laptop to detect malware.

---

## 6. IoT Security

### Definition

IoT Security protects Internet of Things (IoT) devices from cyber attacks.

### Simple Explanation

Smart devices connected to the internet can also become targets for hackers if they are not properly secured.

### Example

Protecting Smart TVs, CCTV cameras, smart locks, and Wi-Fi routers from unauthorized access.

---

## 7. SOC (Security Operations Center)

### Definition

A Security Operations Center (SOC) is a team that monitors and responds to cyber threats 24×7.

### Simple Explanation

SOC analysts continuously monitor systems, investigate alerts, and respond to security incidents to keep an organization safe.

### Example

A bank's SOC team detects suspicious login attempts and blocks the attack immediately.

---

## 8. Digital Forensics

### Definition

Digital Forensics is the process of investigating cyber crimes by collecting and analyzing digital evidence.

### Simple Explanation

It helps identify how an attack happened, who was responsible, and what data was affected.

### Example

Investigating a hacked computer to find evidence of malware or unauthorized access.

---

# 1.7 Basic Cyber Security Concepts

Before learning Ethical Hacking, you should understand these basic concepts.

---

## 1. Server vs Node

### Server

A **Server** is a powerful computer that provides services, applications, or data to other devices.

### Example

A website server stores website files and sends them to users when they visit the website.

---

### Node

A **Node** is any device connected to a network.

### Example

Laptop, Mobile Phone, Printer, Router.

---

### Easy Difference

| Server | Node |
| --- | --- |
| Provides services | Uses services |
| Powerful computer | Any connected device |
| Stores data | Requests data |

---

## 2. Authentication vs Authorization

### Authentication

Authentication verifies **who you are**.

### Example

Logging in using your username and password.

---

### Authorization

Authorization decides **what you are allowed to access**.

### Example

Only an administrator can access the Admin Panel.

---

### Easy Difference

| Authentication | Authorization |
| --- | --- |
| Who are you? | What can you access? |
| Identity Verification | Access Permission |
| Login | User Permissions |

---

## 3. Encryption

### Definition

Encryption converts readable data into an unreadable format to protect it from unauthorized users.

### Simple Explanation

Even if someone steals encrypted data, they cannot read it without the correct decryption key.

### Example

WhatsApp messages are protected using encryption.

---

## 4. Firewall

### Definition

A Firewall is a security system that monitors and filters incoming and outgoing network traffic.

### Simple Explanation

It acts like a security guard by allowing safe traffic and blocking malicious traffic.

### Example

A firewall blocks hackers from accessing a company's internal network.

---

## 5. VPN (Virtual Private Network)

### Definition

A VPN creates a secure and private connection over the internet.

### Simple Explanation

It encrypts internet traffic and protects user privacy, especially on public Wi-Fi networks.

### Example

Using a VPN while working from a coffee shop to keep your internet activity secure.

---

# 1.8 Cyber Security Tool – VirusTotal

## Definition

**VirusTotal** is a free online security tool that scans files and URLs for viruses, malware, and other security threats.

---

## Simple Explanation

Sometimes we download a file or receive a link from someone, but we're not sure if it's safe. Instead of opening it directly, we can upload the file or paste the URL into VirusTotal. It checks the file using multiple antivirus engines and tells us if it is safe or suspicious.

> **Note:** VirusTotal helps detect threats, but it cannot guarantee that every file is 100% safe.
> 

---

## Example

You receive a file named **Invoice.pdf.exe** through email. Before opening it, you upload it to VirusTotal. The scan shows that several antivirus engines detect it as malware, so you delete the file instead of opening it.

---

# 1.9 Basic Protection Tips

Following a few simple security practices can protect you from many cyber attacks.

---

## 1. Use Strong Passwords

### Explanation

Create passwords that are long and difficult to guess. Use a combination of uppercase letters, lowercase letters, numbers, and special characters.

### Example

✅ **Ravi@2026#Secure**

❌ **123456**

---

## 2. Use a Unique Password for Every Account

### Explanation

Never use the same password for multiple accounts. If one account is hacked, your other accounts can also be compromised.

### Example

Use different passwords for Gmail, Instagram, and Banking.

---

## 3. Enable Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)

### Explanation

2FA adds an extra layer of security by asking for an OTP or authentication code after entering your password.

### Example

After entering your Gmail password, Google sends an OTP to your phone for verification.

---

## 4. Avoid Unknown Links

### Explanation

Do not click on suspicious links received through emails, SMS, or social media.

### Example

A fake message says, **"Click here to claim your prize."** Ignore and delete it.

---

## 5. Install Antivirus Software

### Explanation

Antivirus software detects and removes viruses, malware, and other harmful programs.

### Example

Windows Defender scans your computer and blocks infected files.

---

## 6. Keep Software Updated

### Explanation

Software updates fix security vulnerabilities and improve protection against new threats.

### Example

Regularly update Windows, Android, browsers, and installed applications.

---

## 7. Avoid Public Wi-Fi for Sensitive Work

### Explanation

Public Wi-Fi networks are less secure and attackers may try to intercept your data.

### Example

Avoid logging into your bank account while using free airport Wi-Fi.

---

## 8. Take Regular Backups

### Explanation

Keep copies of important files so they can be restored if your system is infected by ransomware or damaged.

### Example

Store important documents on an external hard drive or cloud storage.

---

**Example:** Protecting employee records and customer databases.

---

### 2. Financial Security

Cyber Security helps prevent online fraud and financial loss.

**Example:** Preventing unauthorized online transactions.

---

### 3. Customer Trust

Customers trust companies that keep their personal information secure.

**Example:** An e-commerce website protects customer payment details.

---

**Example:** Banks follow security regulations to protect customer information.

Cyber Security helps businesses continue operating even during cyber attacks.

**Example:** A company restores its data from backups after a ransomware attack.

# 1.11 Hacking vs Ethical Hacking

Many people think hacking and ethical hacking are the same, but the biggest difference is **permission**.

---

## Hacking

### Definition

Hacking is gaining unauthorized access to a computer system or network for illegal purposes.

### Example

A hacker steals customer passwords from a website without permission.

---

## Ethical Hacking

### Definition

Ethical Hacking is the legal process of testing a system's security with the owner's permission.

### Example

A company hires an Ethical Hacker to find security weaknesses before criminals can exploit them.

---

## Difference

| Hacking | Ethical Hacking |
| --- | --- |
| Illegal | Legal |
| No Permission | Written Permission |
| Causes Damage | Improves Security |
| Criminal Activity | Security Testing |

# 1.12 Cyber Security Certifications

Cyber Security certifications help students and professionals improve their knowledge and build a successful career.

---

## 1. CHE (Certified Hacker Expert)

### Definition

CHE is a beginner-level certification that teaches the basics of Ethical Hacking and Cyber Security.

### Simple Explanation

It helps beginners understand networking, security concepts, and ethical hacking fundamentals.

### Best For

Students who are starting their Cyber Security journey.

---

## 2. eJPT (Junior Penetration Tester)

### Definition

eJPT is a practical penetration testing certification for beginners.

### Simple Explanation

It focuses on real-world hacking labs instead of only theory. Students learn information gathering, scanning, exploitation, and web security.

### Best For

Anyone who wants hands-on Ethical Hacking experience.

---

## 3. OSCP (Offensive Security Certified Professional)

### Definition

OSCP is one of the most respected practical Ethical Hacking certifications in the Cyber Security industry.

### Simple Explanation

The exam is completely practical. Candidates must find vulnerabilities and exploit them in a controlled lab environment.

### Famous Quote

**"Try Harder."**

### Best For

Experienced Ethical Hackers and Penetration Testers.

---

## 4. ISO 27001

### Definition

ISO 27001 is an international standard for Information Security Management Systems (ISMS).

### Simple Explanation

It helps organizations protect sensitive information by following proper security policies and risk management.

### Example

A company follows ISO 27001 to protect customer data and confidential business information.

---

## 5. GRC (Governance, Risk & Compliance)

### Definition

GRC focuses on managing security policies, identifying risks, and following legal and regulatory requirements.

### Simple Explanation

Unlike Ethical Hacking, GRC is more focused on security management, documentation, and compliance.

### Example

A bank follows government security regulations to protect customer financial data.

---

# 1.14 Digital Data Life Cycle

Every piece of digital data goes through different stages during its lifetime. Protecting data at every stage is an important part of Cyber Security.

---

## 1. Create

### Explanation

Data is created by users or applications.

### Example

Writing a document or taking a photo.

---

## 2. Store

### Explanation

The created data is saved on a computer, server, or cloud storage.

### Example

Saving files on Google Drive or a laptop.

---

## 3. Use

### Explanation

Users access and work with the stored data.

### Example

Opening and editing a Word document.

---

## 4. Share

### Explanation

Data is shared with other users through email, cloud storage, or messaging applications.

### Example

Sending a project report through Gmail.

---

## 5. Archive / Delete

### Explanation

Old data is either stored for future use or permanently deleted when no longer needed.

### Example

Deleting old files or moving company records to secure backup storage.

---

### Easy Remember

**Create → Store → Use → Share → Archive/Delete**
